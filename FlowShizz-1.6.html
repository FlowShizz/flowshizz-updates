FlowShizz 1.6

Please install this one: websites may not have been blocked

  • Updating FlowShizz could quietly stop it blocking websites. Apps were still blocked and the countdown still ran, so there was nothing to notice — but websites were left open.
  • The part of FlowShizz that edits your Mac's website list runs with special permission, and that permission is tied to the exact copy of the app that asked for it. Replacing the app during an update left it pointing at the old copy, and macOS then refused to start it. FlowShizz now renews that permission whenever it updates, and checks the component actually answers.
  • If it ever can't, FlowShizz says so plainly and asks for your password instead, so the block is still enforced. It will no longer tell you a block is running when part of it isn't.

Setting it up makes sense now

  • Turning on background blocking used to report "Couldn't install" at the exact moment macOS was simply waiting for you to approve it. It now tells you what to do and offers a button that opens the right Settings page.

Blocking is free and always will be. Pro only covers blocking your iPhone and your Mac together.
